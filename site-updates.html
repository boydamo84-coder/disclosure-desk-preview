A new way to investigate: Hypothesis Lab & Research Gap Finder

Disclosure Desk has added a dedicated research workspace. Go beyond reading a case: document possible explanations, challenge your own ideas, find questions worth following up and keep a revision history.

Explore the new Hypothesis LabOpen Investigation Boards
Read the full update and how to use the new tools ⌄

What's new in this research beta?

  • Hypothesis Lab: Write competing explanations for a reported event, including assumptions, predictions, alternative interpretations, sources and evidence that could challenge an idea.
  • Compare: Review different explanations side by side. This helps organise questions; it does not decide which explanation is true.
  • History: Keep track of saved revisions so you can see how your reasoning changes as you investigate.
  • Research Gap Finder: Browse unanswered questions suggested by missing structured information in our curated case catalogue. A catalogue gap is not proof that an original record is missing.
  • Optional private notebook backups: Enable encrypted saving in your browser, then manually back up the encrypted notebook to your signed-in Disclosure Desk account. You can restore it in another browser using your original passphrase.
  • Who’s Who — inspect the original evidence: Read original-source profiles, claim-specific testimony, contradictory reports and open research questions. Disclosure Desk will not assign people or their claims an editorial numerical rating. A private five-criterion community evidence worksheet is being tested separately and is not yet accepting votes.

Getting started — six simple steps

  1. Open Boards and select Hypothesis Lab. You can explore a fictional demonstration first.
  2. Create a notebook and add your first explanation in Write. Add possible tests and source links; record revisions as you work.
  3. Try Compare to examine explanations together, and History to revisit earlier revisions.
  4. Choose Research Gaps to review suggested follow-up questions; save only those you want to investigate.
  5. For local persistence, choose Enable encrypted saving and create a strong passphrase you can remember. Your work is saved locally only after you enable encryption and save completed revisions.
  6. To use encrypted cloud backup, sign into your account, select Save encrypted backup to account and confirm. To recover on a new device, sign in, choose Restore account backup on this device and enter the same passphrase.

Privacy and beta limitations: Cloud backup is optional and manual, not automatic synchronisation. We cannot recover a forgotten passphrase. Keep a separate encrypted download. Until independent security review is complete, do not enter identifying or highly sensitive research. Suggested gaps and hypotheses are research aids, not verified findings.
