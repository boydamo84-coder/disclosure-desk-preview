Community Rules

Investigate openly. Question claims respectfully. Protect witnesses and other researchers.

Published as beta community-policy guidance · 3 November 2026

Current website vs future roadmap: Public witness-report submission, comment discussions, reactions and LIVE hearing rooms are enabled on the official Disclosure Desk website, subject to sign-in, service availability and moderation. Private Witness drafts are separate and never publish themselves. User-created Discussions sections, private one-to-one Chat and research publishing are planned, not yet live. An enabled website control is not a promise that a particular submission or live hearing is available at this moment.

1. Accounts and public names

Guests may explore publicly available news, cases, recordings and approved reports. To submit a public witness report, post comments or LIVE chat, like or dislike content, or flag content, you must be signed in to a registered Disclosure Desk account.

You choose a public username at signup, visible and Chat-discoverable by default after sign-in; you may hide or edit it in Profile. Usernames must not impersonate moderators, government agencies or other people, and must not include contact details or abusive language. Your email address and private research documents are not shown as your public name.

2. Public witness reports and the world map

  1. Sign in, select Witness, and describe what you observed in your own words. Include an approximate date, time and town or city only.
  2. Check your report for personal names, house or street addresses, postcode/ZIP codes, private photos and identifying details before choosing public submission. Do not submit someone else's report without permission.
  3. When the public submission service is available, valid reports are published automatically after basic safety and validation checks—no routine pre-approval wait. A report may be rejected by those checks if it contains sensitive location details or fails submission rules.
  4. Approved-format public reports will appear in the world map's Witness Reports Only filter, using an approximate town/city location rather than an exact sighting or home coordinate. Publication does not mean Disclosure Desk verified the account.

Rate limit: Up to 3 new public witness reports per account in any rolling 24-hour period. The server enforces this, so switching browsers or devices will not reset the limit. Private drafts do not count as public submissions. Duplicate, fabricated, threatening or spam reports may be removed.

3. Likes and dislikes

Public witness reports and community comments can display 👍 Like and 👎 Dislike counters on the official website. Totals are visible to readers, but voting requires a signed-in account. Each account has one active vote per item; you can change your choice or remove it, but you cannot repeatedly add votes. Automated or coordinated vote manipulation is prohibited.

A dislike is not a formal report and will not automatically remove content. Use Flag / Report if something violates these rules.

4. Community flagging and moderation

  1. Only signed-in users can flag a public witness report, comment or chat message. Choose a reason such as spam, abuse, privacy risk, harassment or other rule violation.
  2. One account's flag counts once per item, even if the person returns later. You may not flag your own content, use extra accounts to inflate a flag count or organise malicious mass-reporting.
  3. For public witness reports and comments: content publishes immediately after basic safety checks, without routine manual approval. The first four valid flags record a concern but do not create a routine moderator task. At 5 flags from distinct registered accounts, the reported item becomes temporarily hidden from the public feed (and map for witness reports) pending review. This is not proof the claim is false and it is not a deletion.
  4. The normal moderator queue focuses on hidden items, not every single flag. Moderators can restore, continue hiding or reject content after review. For immediate safety risks, including personal information, threats or unlawful content, contact the safety team directly using the support address below. Malicious mass-reporting is prohibited.

A vote never counts as a flag. Detailed abuse reports can also be sent to support@uapdisclosuredesk.com. Avoid quoting or reposting sensitive material in an abuse report.

5. Comments and LIVE hearing chat

Discuss the evidence, question sources and share alternative explanations without targeting individuals. Do not post threats, discriminatory abuse, stalking details, advertisements, fabricated citations, repeated spam or private witness information. Make clear when a claim is unverified.

Signed-in registered users can comment immediately on News, Archive case files, Videos, and Watch & Read titles (documentaries, films, books and podcast episodes). Guests may read approved comments. Each discussion uses the same self-moderation flagging rules. Registered users may post to LIVE hearing chat only while its officially verified event room is open. Approved LIVE messages remain available as a read-only transcript beside the official recording after the hearing; guests may read the transcript without seeing private account details. Messages can be flagged, and five distinct valid registered flags temporarily hide them for review. Moderators can also hide messages for urgent privacy or safety concerns.

Planned: user-created Discussions sections (not yet live)

The proposed Discussions tab will let registered users create public topic sections, post and reply, and share relevant external sources. A section's creator becomes a limited steward for that section, not an unrestricted moderator. Guests will be able to read approved public content; publishing and voting will require a registered account.

Stewardship is not gatekeeping: stewards may organise and pin useful posts, manage their section description, flag violations and escalate concerns directly to the site owner. They will not be able to ban people for disagreement, impose preapproval on normal posts, or unilaterally permanently delete another member's contributions. The overall site administrator retains final review, can revoke a steward role and can reverse inappropriate actions. The usual five-distinct-flags temporary-hide rule will apply to routine violations; urgent safety and privacy risks should be reported directly to the site owner without waiting for five flags.

Private 1-to-1 Chat

New members choose a public username at signup. Once they sign in, that username is public and discoverable for Chat requests by default. Email addresses remain private. Members can hide their username or switch off Chat requests in Profile at any time; existing preferences remain unchanged.

In the Chat tab, a registered member may send one introductory invitation to another available public username. The recipient chooses Accept or Decline; no further messages may be sent before acceptance. There are no group chats.

Members can block or report unwanted contact. Repeated unsolicited requests, harassment, coercion and attempts to reveal private research or witness information are prohibited. Public discoverability never means consent to a conversation.

6. Community-published W.NE research

Your W.NE documents and Investigation Boards remain private by default. A future Publish to Community action will require your deliberate selection and a clear preview of the public copy; creating or saving a document will never publish it automatically.

Research submissions should clearly distinguish primary sources, factual observations, hypotheses and unresolved questions. Credit other authors and publishers, avoid copyrighted material you lack rights to share, and remove private notes and identifying information before submitting. The planned community library will allow readers to review and rate published research, with reporting and moderation safeguards. Research publication is separate from the automatic witness-report process and may have an additional review step.

7. Privacy, corrections and appeals

Keep locations approximate. Never post another person's full name, address, phone number, account details, medical information or private communications without a lawful basis and appropriate permission. Do not use Disclosure Desk to identify or contact a witness against their wishes.

Registered authors can manage their own contributions from My Research Hub where available; some edits have safety limits, and deleting a witness report does not reset its rolling 24-hour submission allowance. Research Studio cloud documents have a separate recoverable trash process. Authors can request help correcting or removing their material. If your content is hidden, you may ask for a review with the relevant report or discussion reference. We may retain limited safety or moderation records as explained in the Privacy Policy.

For corrections, moderation questions or an appeal, contact support@uapdisclosuredesk.com. Give the report or comment link and a brief explanation; do not send sensitive witness details.

These rules may be updated as community features are tested. Proposed Discussions and private Chat rules are illustrative until separately approved, implemented and published. Changes to thresholds or posting permissions will be reflected here before public activation.

Watch & Read additions

Registered members can add public documentary, film, book and podcast links. Valid additions appear automatically; duplicate titles and links are rejected. Inclusion does not verify a claim or endorse a creator. Report inappropriate content honestly: one account counts once, and five distinct registered reports hide a title for owner review. The owner can restore or remove it. Do not organise abusive or misleading reports.
