## Method and editorial boundaries

Six original Who's Who professional/publication figures gain narrowly defined **provisional research notes**, with the same existing reader pattern as the prior 12 claim/recommendation drafts. Every note links independently inspectable original journal articles, broadcaster transcripts, official records or named publishing outlets where available, states the source's scope and includes alternative interpretations and key evidentiary gaps. Attribution of an interview or written paper does not prove any claimed extraordinary event. Criticising a claim's evidence does not assign moral qualities, honesty or general competence to the individual.

### 6. Kevin Knuth — knuth-2019-nimitz-acceleration-estimates

**Precisely attributed proposition:** Kevin Knuth and coauthors published 2019 estimates of extreme minimum accelerations for reported UAP manoeuvres, including Nimitz encounter accounts, conditional on reported distances and time intervals.

**Scope:** The paper's calculations are real published mathematical estimates. Their relevance to the actual object's dynamics depends on the accuracy of reported motion inputs; peer-reviewed publication is not access to raw 2004 radar telemetry or proof of extraterrestrial origin.

**Outstanding verification:** Audit exact source of each motion input, especially personally communicated radar recollections, and contrast reported geometry with full 2004 original timestamps, platform positioning, released tracks and independent reconstructions. Recompute interval-dependent g values and uncertainty ranges; do not treat a conditional calculation or a witness's professional standing as proof of physical acceleration.

**Primary statement/paper:** [Original 2019 Entropy paper, Knuth–Powell–Reali](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/21/10/939). Publisher open-access research article and explicit modelling assumptions; computed accelerations should not be reported as directly calibrated sensor measurements.

## Five important anti-overclaim findings

1. An official confirmation that Navy personnel recorded a clip (Corbell/Omaha) **does not** independently corroborate a spacecraft's speed, underwater propulsion or exotic origin.
2. Ross Coulthart's reporting, Grusch's testimony and a critical TV analysis may examine the same secondhand allegation. They cannot be counted as three independent recoveries or samples.
3. Steven Greer's 2001 press event and contemporaneous reporting independently establish a public news event; testimony from many speakers still requires individual first-party incident validation.
4. Nolan and Vallée authored the **same** 2022 sample-analysis paper. The study found no significant nonterrestrial isotope differences in tested subsamples, and the physical source remains uncertain. **Never double-count the joint paper** or promote unknown provenance into evidence of non-human manufacture. For precise wording consult the journal's original article 100788.
5. Knuth's estimated flight accelerations are derived from a published conditional mathematical model using witness and report inputs. The calculations are **not** publicly verified, direct platform telemetry for the 2004 Nimitz encounter.
