## Why this index exists

All **50** Who's Who people now have at least one expanded, publicly inspectable source-reader on the isolated feature branch. Previously there were **27 official NASA/congressional role cards** (56 source entries) and **7 public-claim or research-recommendation drafts** (27 source entries). This checkpoint adds **17 original-publication/work/source indexes** (37 source entries). Because Mike Gold appears in both the NASA and claim/recommendation sets, there are **50 unique profiles** rather than 51 separate people. **120 citations/source entries** in all, including repeated original roster pages and first-party links—NOT 120 independently corroborated incidents.

This is not a verdict on anyone's honesty, nor a 0–100 score. First-person testimony shows what was said. An archive indexes what was published. A peer-reviewed paper may support a *specific* methodology and measurement, but does not validate unrelated UAP theories. An official institutional role and documentary credit are never evidence that a recovered extraordinary vehicle exists. Every work record has `status: research_links_indexed` and must be separately fact-checked, including original-media dates, bias/context, counterarguments, witness independence, source rights and accessibility. `checkedAt` should not be confused with a science or authenticity clearance; work records use **link indexed**.

**Editorial distinction:** source-class labels distinguish publisher/distributor, author's first-party index, independently identified institution, and third-party archive. First-party reporting and show marketing can verify the person's own publication/appearance but must NOT be counted as independent corroboration of its claims. The UI lets readers open original material via a collapsed `Inspect original publications & source trail` section, while remaining **Not assessed** for any unreviewed incident. No member vote or site rating can be assigned to a publication list.

## Specific source/counterargument safeguards

- **Bob Lazar:** A preserved 1989 KLAS interview or transcript establishes his claims existed by that date, not his degrees, S-4 employment, reverse-engineering programme, or a non-human craft. The original reporter's retrospective describes unresolved academic records and should not be selectively quoted as either blanket approval or categorical refutation.
- **Jake Barber:** First-party broadcaster interview is directly attributable and the date recorded, but video provenance, chain of custody, linked contractors, independent corroboration and the true identity of any object remain outside this record.
- **Larry Warren:** The 1997 book is traceable through the British National Bibliography. Modern Warren/Longero interview and separate Charles Halt interview are **disagreeing first-person accounts**; neither is a substitute for original December 1980 MoD documents. The National Archives' Halt memorandum is not automatic confirmation of Warren's different account.
- **Steven Greer:** Two independent contemporary newspapers confirm the 2001 press conference happened and Greer organised it. Neither authenticates all speakers' claims or alleged recovered materials.
- **Mick West:** A sceptical reconstruction needs original camera settings, coordinate geometry, uncertainties and competing analysis. A persuasive explanation is not automatically a uniquely proven object identification.
- **Avi Loeb and Kevin Knuth:** Original institutional records confirm the research programmes, not discovery of non-human engineering. In particular, the UAlbany article says most observations were explained, one ambiguity remained, and no evidence of non-human intelligence was found.
- **Garry Nolan:** Pathology/faculty work and Sol Foundation activities are distinct; institutional affiliation does not validate any unusual specimen without accessible laboratory evidence and replicable testing.
