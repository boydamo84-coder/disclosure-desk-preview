# Disclosure Desk — M29 human editorial approval and claim-evidence rubric (DRAFT)
**Prepared:** 16 November 2026 • **Scope:** the 23 individually attributed Who's Who claim/recommendation dossiers.  
**Status:** RESEARCH HANDOFF ONLY — no individual score or community vote has been approved. The 27 role/public-work-only profiles do not receive an event-evidence score.

## 1. What the numbers would mean — and what they must not imply

If the gates below are fully satisfied in a future authorised release, a **Disclosure Desk editorial 0–100 indicator** can communicate **the strength and completeness of independently inspectable support for one narrowly described public claim**. It is never a probability that an unidentified object is alien, a percentage of a person's honesty, a psychological assessment, or a rank of public figures. An official publication proves statements and office actions occurred; it does not prove everything a witness said was true.

A **separate signed-in community 0–100 rating**, with a voter count, would describe participating users' opinions on the **public evidence for the same precise claim**, not the person. No anonymous/person-wide believability vote is authorised. Community values must be independently labelled and must never be shown as the site's editorial assessment.

Some claims (notably general policy, open records and scientific advocacy) have **no meaningful physical-incident percentage**, and must display **Not applicable**, never 0/100. For other cases, missing or inaccessible raw records may mean **Not assessed**, not an artificial zero or an automatic 50. No calculation or number should be published until every required source and person-level authorisation gate is genuinely met.

## 2. Existing rubric: five evidence criteria, maximum 100

The pre-existing \`dd-claim-evidence-v1\` public UI already defines these potential maximum weights. M29 documents them for human consideration but sets **no points in any criterion**.

| Criterion | Maximum if approved later | What a human editor must document |
|---|---:|---|
| Original claim recorded in context | 25 | Exact primary publication, writer/speaker, date, original accessible recording or document; context and whether the key passage is first- or secondhand |
| Independent corroboration | 30 | Distinct original evidence origin and demonstrably different witness, measurement or document chain. Do **not** count coauthors, syndicated stories, different videos of the same report, a shared confidential witness or the same hearing container as separate material recoveries |
| Inspectable supporting records/data | 25 | Dated original records, instrument metadata, calibration, chain of custody, direct measurements and lawful independent access appropriate for the claim |
| Specificity and independent testability | 10 | Defined assertion, dates/locations, falsifiable hypotheses, original data and feasible repeat checks; no credit simply because a claim is sensational |
| Alternative explanations and contrary evidence | 10 | Specific credible original contrasting records, model uncertainty, competing interpretations, case-specific agency analyses, missing evidence and any unresolved disagreement |

These are **upper bounds, not points automatically awarded**. The editorial template must provide **claim-specific source IDs and a reasoned finding for each criterion**. If the original material is not available or qualified verification cannot be completed, the correct outcome is an explicit hold, not a guessed point total. Scores should not be directly comparable across incommensurate categories without methods caveats.

## 5. Human editorial workflow — mandatory before any scored publication

**Stage A — prepare the source packet (researcher, NOT signoff).** Fix one narrowly stated claim and primary document or originating broadcast. Add exact page/timecode and original context, an accessible source or honest access denial, verbatim/source-limited description, initial alternative accounts, lineage comparison and material not available. Mark any reprint as the same source. Ask whether an event score is even meaningful.

**Stage B — first qualified human editor.** Independently obtain/inspect original primary evidence. Manually review source authenticity, date, distinctness, countersources, original measurements and why each proposed rubric criterion could or could not be supported. State limitations. Provide clear rationale and source IDs. Mark cases with inaccessible underlying materials as **hold/not assessed** unless a justified narrower attribution-only rubric is explicitly approved.

**Stage C — a separate qualified second human editor.** A genuinely different person reviews the original primary documents and competing evidence, checks the claimed independence of sources, tests the suggested interpretation and marks agreements/disagreements. The second reviewer cannot be a script, a duplicate account controlled by the same editor, a fabricated name or a model claiming to be human. Record conflict-of-interest disclosure and revision history. Unresolved differences must block the public score or be resolved through a documented further expert review.

**Stage D — publication approval and freeze.** Before any site score change: independent human signatures; approved claim wording, versioned five-criterion rationale linked to original sources, owner authorisation, copyright/portrait audit, and security test results for any community feature. Review the exact current production/R3 branch and complete mobile/desktop navigation regression; prepare rollback. Publish only the approved claim/version. Maintain corrections and a clear right to revise if new evidence emerges.

**Stage E — community voting (separate security programme).** Independently test real signed-in users with distinct JWT sessions, RLS enforcement, user-to-user isolation, uniqueness, abusive voting limits, cooldown/rate restrictions, deleted users, privacy policy, voter-count integrity and role scope. Never enable voting merely because editorial materials exist. Previous exploratory SQL is intentionally excluded from the public release package. Owner authorisation is required separately.
