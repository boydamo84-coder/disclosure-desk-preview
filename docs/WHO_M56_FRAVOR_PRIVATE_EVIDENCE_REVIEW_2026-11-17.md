## The question being assessed

**Narrow question:** How strongly do *currently accessible and separately attributable* records substantiate Fravor's reported November 2004 Nimitz visual encounter and the claimed unusual physical manoeuvres?

This is **NOT** a rating of David Fravor's honesty, character, integrity, professional competence, or how likely an extraterrestrial origin is. A correctly reproduced witness statement does not by itself independently establish the described object, speed, acceleration or provenance. Distinct claims may require separate future scorecards; do not treat all aspects of the encounter as a single verified proposition.

## Revised evidence units — separately review, do not infer one case score

**Correction following a scope challenge (17 November 2026):** The initial blended **54/100** is **WITHDRAWN** as an active score. It measured public access to full radar/FLIR measurements alongside the quality of multiple original witness accounts and official Navy footage, and could be misread as claiming the whole 2004 reported event had weak support. It is retained **only as an obsolete private audit calculation** below, not as a rating to publish or a numerical conclusion.

The source evidence must instead be assessed under **five separate propositions**, each with its own provenance, limits, independent-editor verification and (only if appropriate) a separately justified evidence-strength rating:

1. **Visual encounter:** Fravor and Dietrich gave separately attributable first-person reports from the same sortie. Their combined testimony is substantial evidence of a reported unidentified encounter, but not an independently measured trajectory.
2. **Radar accounts:** Fravor testified that Princeton, Nimitz and E-2 systems tracked an object; the same testimony says his own fire-control radar never picked it up. The 2019 Knuth/Powell/Reali article discusses Kevin Day's distinct radar-operator account and offers conditional estimates **without access to original radar tracks**. Reported distinct radar systems merit their own evidentiary analysis, not a dismissive score.
3. **Later official FLIR footage:** The Navy's November 2004 footage was officially released by DoD. Navy public-affairs documents discuss circulation aboard Nimitz. This is genuine instrument footage, but recorded by another crew later and **not independently proven to depict the identical target** Fravor saw.
4. **Quantitative extraordinary kinematics:** The reported sixty-mile movement, altitude changes and rapid apparent departures deserve precise technical research. Validation requires original range, time and sensor state/geometry data, uncertainty and independent replication. Conditional modelling or later video alone does not settle those inputs.
5. **Nonhuman/extraterrestrial origin:** No current record here establishes that identification; it must not be inferred from the case remaining unexplained, and should not be assigned a default 0, 100 or probability figure.

## Original and contextual source ledger

**1. Official first-person congressional testimony**  
[U.S. Government Publishing Office — 26 July 2023 House UAP hearing](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-118hhrg53022/html/CHRG-118hhrg53022.htm), statement at HTML lines ~830–901 and Langworthy questioning ~2413–2473. **Access:** original first-party HTML checked at relevant passages. **Establishes:** Fravor testified that his crew saw a white elongated object moving over water. He attributed a later distant-position report to a controller and said a different crew recorded the released footage. He also said his own aircraft radar did not capture the object. **Does NOT establish:** independently measured 60-mile travel, radar jamming, physical acceleration or object identity. Transcript and committee-hosted mirror are one hearing family, not two observations.

**2. Second named pilot from the SAME flight**  
[CBS 60 Minutes — original 16 May 2021 broadcast article/transcript](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ufo-military-intelligence-60-minutes-2021-05-16/). **Access:** publisher transcript passages checked. **Establishes:** Alex Dietrich offers a separately attributable perspective from the same flying event; the journalistic interview also contains Fravor's retelling. **Does NOT establish:** distinct second incident or complete calibrated measurements. CBS is an interview publisher, not a radar laboratory.

**3. Official November 2004 video release provenance**  
[U.S. Department of Defense — 27 April 2020 release](https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/). **Access:** official indexed release title and summary confirmed; direct site fetch returned HTTP 403, so no complete original-document passage audit is claimed in this pass. **Establishes:** DoD publicly authorised three Navy videos, one from November 2004 and two from January 2015. **Does NOT establish:** Fravor's original visual observation filmed on the same sortie, radar-tracked extraordinary motion or an alien explanation.

**4. A different sortie, a different observer and an original interview**  
[New York Magazine / Intelligencer — 2019 Chad Underwood interview](https://nymag.com/intelligencer/2019/12/tic-tac-ufo-video-q-and-a-with-navy-pilot-chad-underwood.html). **Access:** publisher original relevant passages checked; interview describes recording about an hour after Fravor's encounter. **Establishes:** Underwood is publicly identified as the later infrared recorder. **Does NOT establish:** identical physical target across sorties or independently verified acceleration. Do not count this footage as continuous observation of Fravor's manoeuvre.

**5. General scientific data sufficiency, NOT a case verdict**  
[NASA UAP FAQs](https://science.nasa.gov/uap/faqs/), especially Q4–Q7. **Access:** original NASA page checked. **Establishes:** limited high-quality measurements constrain scientific evaluation and NASA's UAP study was about methods, not an adjudication of every historical UAP case. **Does NOT establish:** whether the Nimitz account is true or false.

**6. Competing camera-behaviour hypothesis, NOT a final incident determination**  
[Mick West — original Metabunk September 2019 FLIR1 discussion](https://www.metabunk.org/threads/are-the-navy-ufos-real-or-just-in-the-low-information-zone-the-liz.10921/). **Access:** original named author's post checked. **Claim:** loss of tracking and camera zoom can explain seemingly abrupt end-of-clip target movement; test this against sensor modes and original imagery. **Does NOT establish:** a resolution of Fravor/Dietrich visual descriptions or any missing radar record. A competing model is evidence of an alternative interpretation, not proof that it is correct.

## Additional sources brought into the scope recalibration

**7. Peer-reviewed study that includes radar-operator reporting**

This is documentary video provenance, not a sensor-calibrated analysis.

## Four critical distinctions for the future public reader

1. **Attribution:** We have a clear official record of what Fravor testified. This must never automatically become proof of physical acceleration.
2. **Corroboration:** A named second witness may provide a separately attributable account **of one shared event**. Published repeats and shared hearing footage do not multiply independent witnesses.
3. **Video:** The released 2004 Navy footage was captured during **a later sortie**, so comparisons need a documented link and original metadata before claiming the object is identical.
4. **Motion/identity:** Original full time-aligned radar, platform position, FLIR master and uncertainty bounds are not available in this reviewed source set. An unexplained report is not proof of a nonhuman origin.
