# Three Range Fouler reports: similar reporting, different observations

**Disclosure Desk editorial research · AI-assisted draft · Version 0.2 · 27 November 2026**

Category: Case analysis | Status: staged for human editorial review; not published.

## Abstract
PURSUE Release 04 contains three Range Fouler reporting forms dated by the catalogue to 2019–2020, each paired with a separate video listing. Comparing the visible witness narratives reveals an important contrast: two describe small objects with difficult-to-establish geometry, while the third describes something that appeared balloon-like and moved with the wind. Similar reporting procedures should not be mistaken for evidence of a single type of phenomenon.

All three official descriptions name their corresponding debrief. AARO explicitly distinguishes these informational descriptions from analytical conclusions. The PR106 duration difference is a narrow, reproducible metadata question, not evidence that any material was intentionally concealed. The listings use year-start dates; do not treat those entries as exact event times for environmental analysis.

## The crossover
The forms share a reporting method: request the contact location, direction, environmental conditions, apparent form and a narrative. Each asks for recording preservation. This offers a useful common framework for comparing the reported observations. However, the accounts differ in the apparent shapes, motion and the circumstances under which the observations were made.

D091 is a particularly useful comparison case for testing how ordinary airborne objects can enter a UAP reporting workflow. A balloon-like appearance and wind-following motion make a conventional explanation plausible, but the witness did not establish that identity. Conversely, D090's reported high apparent speed is not a calibrated ground-speed measurement. Without verified geometry and motion of the observing platform, apparent motion is insufficient to establish extraordinary performance.

## What is supported and what is not
The released forms document three reports and their different descriptions. They do not establish a common object or repeated technology. Redacted coordinates, times and other fields restrict environmental comparison. A missing field cannot be treated as a negative observation. A video and debrief associated with one event are companion records, not automatically independent sightings.

## Reproducible next steps
The official video descriptions and associated report identifiers are now confirmed. Inspect each available recording and obtain publicly released sensor metadata, if available. Ask the publisher to clarify PR106’s stated footage duration versus the listed duration and whether an unaltered original or processing history can be released. Log what remains unknown. Compare each video to its own narrative before attempting cross-case comparison. Ask for releasable calibrated range, platform-motion and environmental information rather than guessing flight paths from redactions. For environmental analysis, wait for verified event time and location. Maintain separate hypotheses (balloon or other windborne object, conventional aircraft or drone, viewing geometry, unresolved) without assigning invented probabilities.
