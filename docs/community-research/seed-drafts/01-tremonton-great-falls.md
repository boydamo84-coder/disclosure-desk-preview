# Two UFO films, one ambiguous reel: Tremonton and Great Falls

**Disclosure Desk editorial research · AI-assisted draft · Version 0.1 · 26 November 2026**  
Category: Historical research | Status: staged for human editorial review; not published.

## Abstract
The Great Falls (1950) and Tremonton (1952) motion-picture cases are connected in two distinct ways: a later CIA historical account records their joint review by the Robertson Panel, and a National Archives audiovisual finding aid records an uncertain reel attribution. The latter is a concrete provenance question that deserves resolution before researchers compare surviving copies.

## The record trail
The National Archives RG 341 Project Blue Book film inventory lists case 1003323 for Great Falls, Montana, 15 August 1950, and case 1003345 for Tremonton, Utah, 2 July 1952. Its numbered film-list pages 2–3 note that a black-and-white reel associated with the Great Falls case number has a Tremonton marking and might belong to the Utah file [1]. This is an archival qualification, not proof that footage was intentionally mislabeled.

CIA historian Gerald K. Haines reports that the January 1953 Robertson Panel examined both films and attributed the Utah images to reflections from birds and the Montana images to reflections from military aircraft [2, printed p. 72]. This establishes a common official review, not a shared physical cause.

Disclosure Desk's PURSUE catalogue provides additional leads: Release 05 lists a 1953 analysis of unidentified objects associated with Montana and Utah (DOW-UAP-D098); Release 06 indexes the Tremonton Blue Book file (D102), photograph file (D103), and film (PR159). These entries could help trace the record lineage. Their original contents have not been independently inspected in this review [3–6].

## Evidence assessment
- Documented connection: both films were discussed in the same panel review.
- Documented uncertainty: the archive's own finding aid qualifies the attribution of one monochrome reel.
- Research hypothesis: some copies or derivative clips could have inherited an ambiguous attribution. This has not been demonstrated for any particular published clip.
- No evidence here establishes that the two events involved the same object, technology, or origin.

## Reproducible next steps
Request the current National Archives item-level descriptions and any transfer or correction records for both case numbers. Compare reel leader inscriptions, original accession identifiers, format, and sequence against the surviving digitizations. Record each copy's lineage and avoid counting a duplicate as independent evidence. Ask whether a later finding aid resolved the ambiguity. Where permission permits, compare selected frames and record the exact source and time index; do not infer distance or velocity from image resemblance alone.
