# Two UFO films, one ambiguous reel: Tremonton and Great Falls

**Disclosure Desk editorial research · AI-assisted draft · Version 0.2 · 27 November 2026**

Category: Historical research | Status: staged for human editorial review; not published.

## Abstract
The Great Falls (1950) and Tremonton (1952) motion-picture cases are connected in two distinct ways: a later CIA historical account records their joint review by the Robertson Panel, and a National Archives audiovisual finding aid records an uncertain reel attribution. The latter is a concrete provenance question that deserves resolution before researchers compare surviving copies.

## The record trail
The National Archives RG 341 Project Blue Book film inventory lists case 1003323 for Great Falls, Montana, 15 August 1950, and case 1003345 for Tremonton, Utah, 2 July 1952. Its numbered film-list pages 2–3 note that a black-and-white reel associated with the Great Falls case number has a Tremonton marking and might belong to the Utah file [1]. This is an archival qualification, not proof that footage was intentionally mislabeled.

CIA historian Gerald K. Haines reports that the January 1953 Robertson Panel examined both films and attributed the Utah images to reflections from birds and the Montana images to reflections from military aircraft [2, printed p. 72]. This establishes a common official review, not a shared physical cause.

Disclosure Desk's PURSUE catalogue provides additional leads: Release 05 lists a 1953 analysis of unidentified objects associated with Montana and Utah (DOW-UAP-D098); Release 06 indexes the Tremonton Blue Book file (D102), photograph file (D103), and film (PR159). These entries could help trace the record lineage. The three document files remain catalogue leads; their contents could not be retrieved in the 27 November source check [3–5].

## The September 2026 digitisation

The AARO description of PR159 on the official DVIDS release page identifies it as a scan of a colour 16mm print. According to that description, unrelated sewing-machine material appears after approximately 55 seconds, and Idaho landscape material associated with the original submission is absent. AARO suggests possible reuse or compilation of the surviving print, without establishing when or how it happened [6]. This is a separate indication that surviving copies merit provenance checks. It does not establish that PR159 is the monochrome reel qualified in the National Archives inventory. Only the release-page metadata and description were inspected; the footage itself was not reviewed.

## Evidence assessment
- Documented connection: both films were discussed in the same panel review.
- Documented uncertainty: the archive's own finding aid qualifies the attribution of one monochrome reel.
- Documented publisher description: AARO identifies unrelated material in a colour print digitisation in 2026; the relationship to the earlier inventory entry is not established.
- Research hypothesis: some copies or derivative clips could have inherited an ambiguous attribution. This has not been demonstrated for any particular published clip.
- No evidence here establishes that the two events involved the same object, technology, or origin.

## Reproducible next steps
Request the current National Archives item-level descriptions and any transfer or correction records for both case numbers. Compare reel leader inscriptions, original accession identifiers, format, and sequence against the surviving digitizations. Record each copy's lineage and avoid counting a duplicate as independent evidence. Ask whether a later finding aid resolved the ambiguity. Request the accession identifier for PR159 and any transfer or physical inspection records connecting it to the archival inventory; do not assume the colour print and monochrome reel are the same item. Where permission permits, compare selected frames and record the exact source and time index; do not infer distance or velocity from image resemblance alone.
