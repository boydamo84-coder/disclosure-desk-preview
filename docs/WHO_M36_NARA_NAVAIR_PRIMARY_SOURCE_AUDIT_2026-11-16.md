## Discovery 1 — we can now **see** the 1954 → 1956 change

M35 found a plausible May 1988 National Archives report revision on two private historic research collections. M36 goes beyond searchable text: we opened and visually inspected **all six relevant pages**, 1987 and 1988, in a [historical MUFON correspondence scan](https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/ARCHIVES-NATL.pdf):

| Original report edition | PDF page indices, zero-based | What was visually verified |
|---|---|---|
| **22 July 1987** (original) | **54, 55, 56** | Original NARA-branded document header and signature. Paragraph/item **9** on **index 55** labels the civil-defence folder **“Special Meeting July 16, 1954”**; this 1954 date is *not* marked as a correction. |
| **9 May 1988** (revised) | **57, 58, 59** | NARA-branded heading **“Reference Report on MJ-12 (revised)”**, explicitly noting **underlined revisions** to 22 July 1987 report. Item **9** on **index 58** clearly displays **“Special Meeting July 16, 1956”**, with **1956 underlined** in the original scan. Signature on **index 59** identifies **Jo Ann Williamson**, Military Reference Branch. |

The modern [NARA MJ-12 webpage](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos) likewise uses **1956** in its analogous item 9. **The specific change is visibly present in the historical 1988 revised document.** This is stronger evidence of *a historical version change* than the previous text-extraction-only hypothesis.

**Limit:** These two six-page runs are in a *third-party archive* of NARA-attributed documents, **not a directly NARA-hosted/certified May 1988 original**. Even though the scan shows an official-looking heading and signature, M36 does **not** claim NARA officially confirmed the copy's chain of custody or the revision history. The separate [government-hosted Reagan Library collection](https://reaganlibrary.archives.gov/public/2021-06/40-654-209237726-004-008-2021.pdf) remains evidence for the July 1987 publication, but its screenshot service redirected and was unavailable in earlier work.

The change refers to **a civil-defence meeting folder**, not evidence for an MJ-12 government programme. The 1987/1988 reports, their modern government summary and the two private mirrors all derive from one NARA research lineage: they are **not separate independent investigations of extraterrestrial technology**. The report's particular concerns about the alleged Cutler–Twining memo do not establish forensic testing of every purported 1947 Hillenkoetter or 1954 SOM1-01 document.

## Discovery 2 — a direct 26 March 2018 U.S. Navy EO/IR review email

A separately published, [direct original NAVAIR FOIA PDF](https://www.navair.navy.mil/foia/sites/g/files/jejdrs566/files/document/%5Bfilename%5D/2020-006828%20email%202%20AFOSI.pdf) has a **26 March 2018** message about two Navy cockpit videos. We read the **original first PDF page image** (zero-based page 0) and the second page image (zero-based page 1). It says electro-optical/infrared reviewers considered **FLIR1 and GoFast unclassified from that specialty's perspective**. It also says certain cockpit symbology had been reviewed and marks multiple other details and attachments **redacted**.

**What it supports:** A dated, first-party record of *a particular aspect of a Navy security/classification review*. Its statements do **not** prove the video was freely publishable as a complete package at that moment; do not conflate **EO/IR classification status** with other security, FOIA, privacy or final public release approvals.

**What it does not support:** No visual reconstruction of the underlying sensor target, no explanation of what the object was, no speed or acceleration measurement, and no 2004 raw camera master or radar dataset. Treat this as a *governance record*, not independent performance evidence.

## Discovery 3 — detailed Navy FOIA response from July 2025

A [government-authored NAVAIR response letter dated 8 July 2025](https://documents2.theblackvault.com/documents/navy/2020-007231.pdf), mirrored publicly by **The Black Vault**, addresses FOIA **DON-NAVY-2020-007231**, requested **8 April 2020**, about the decisions and actions involved in adding three videos to the official NAVAIR FOIA site. We visually inspected the letter's **PDF pages 1–2**, which state:

- **Four email groups, 34 pages**: released as groups described by Navy as emails 1, 2, 3 and 18.
- **59 emails and 3 legal memoranda, 498 pages**: withheld in entirety under the exemptions cited in the response.
- **Seven documents, 151 pages**: referred to the Office of the Secretary of Defense for a separate review and response, **not counted as accessible exhibits here**.

The original government letter references deliberative-process/attorney-client and personal-privacy exemptions. The **page totals are counts in this specific FOIA response**, not an independent census of all Navy UAP files. **Withholding is not evidence of nonhuman craft, extraordinary physics, or a secret object by itself.** The referred 151 pages' present release status was not checked; do not silently treat them as available or permanently unreleased.

**Host caveat:** This is a **reproduced official Navy letter hosted at a third-party FOIA repository**, not a directly accessible equivalent on navair.navy.mil. Its printed institutional letterhead, FOIA tracking, authorship and visible page structure were checked; no Navy-provided cryptographic original hash was located.
