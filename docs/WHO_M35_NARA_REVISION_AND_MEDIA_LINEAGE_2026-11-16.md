## Main source breakthrough: May 1988 revision explains the 1954/1956 text mismatch

At M34, we verified the original **22 July 1987 National Archives Military Reference Branch** [Reference Report on MJ-12](https://reaganlibrary.archives.gov/public/2021-06/40-654-209237726-004-008-2021.pdf), reproduced in the **official Reagan Presidential Library digitised correspondence collection**. Its paragraph **9**, extracted text lines **2777–2781**, says the civil-defence meeting folder was **“Special Meeting July 16, 1954”**.

The current [official National Archives Project BLUE BOOK / MJ-12 page](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos) reproduces the corresponding paragraph as **“Special Meeting July 16, 1956”** (HTML line 79). The two texts are not identical. The 1954/1956 dates are the dates of a **separate civil-defence meeting folder**, NOT the date of an authenticated MJ-12 project or special committee.

### New evidence between those versions: explicitly revised NARA report of 9 May 1988

Two research-archive collections contain a document identifying itself as:

- **“National Archives — Date May 9, 1988 — Subject: Reference Report on MJ-12 (revised)”**.
- It explicitly says **parts of the July 22, 1987 report were revised and underlined**.
- Its own paragraph **9** states **“Special Meeting July 16, 1956”**, the same wording and year used today by NARA.

**Research source 1:** [Carter UFO 3 archival compilation, historical 9 December 1996 fax](https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/15124.pdf), searchable PDF lines **1888–1928**, with the 1988 report's introduction at **1890–1899** and numbered item 9 at **1911–1915**. The fax header identifies **NNR-A/JFK** in December 1996. This PDF is hosted on a **non-government archival compilation site**, not the NARA domain. It displays the document but is not itself a new archival investigation or a formal certification of source custody.

**Research source 2:** [MUFON's Bill Moore archival correspondence collection (ARCHIVES-NATL.pdf)](https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/ARCHIVES-NATL.pdf) independently exposes a text-indexed **May 9, 1988 revised report** under the National Archives heading. This is a **second circulating copy of the same alleged revised report, not an independent historical investigation**, and its page images and original custody have not been fully authenticated. A [1990 published journal reference](https://thedailydialectics.com/pdfs/space/MUFON-UFO-Journal-Super-Pack-%281967-2008%2C-the-first-40-years%2C-485-Issues%29/MUFON-UFO-Journal-1990-4.-April/MUFON-UFO-Journal-1990-4.-April.pdf) also mentions the May 1988 Williamson revision, but a bibliographic reference is not certification of the actual correction.

**Conservative conclusion:** The 1988 expressly revised version is a compelling and much better-documented **candidate explanation** of the 1954-to-1956 change. What previously looked like an accidental modern transcription difference may instead be NARA's **historical 1988 revision, reflected by the modern webpage**. We have **not** obtained a directly NARA-hosted 1988 original, a page-visual verification of the underlined corrections, a marked-up revision register or a certified official publication history. **Do not upgrade** this from `revision_hypothesis_supported_but_original_1988_version_not_custody_authenticated` to a conclusively certified archival correction.

The correction has **nothing to do with proving the alleged MJ-12 programme**. The revised report continues to identify archival anomalies with the **Cutler–Twining 1954 memo**, including missing Top Secret register number, absence of related NSC records and Cutler's recorded overseas trip. These NARA reports apply to that specific purported memo; no forensic authenticity evaluation of all claimed **1947 Hillenkoetter** or **SOM1-01** variants was completed.

## Navy Nimitz — one 2004 video, multiple containers

The [DoD's 27 April 2020 release](https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/2165713/statement-by-the-department-of-defense-on-the-release-of-historical-navy-videos/) says the public set contains **one Navy video recorded November 2004** and **two recorded January 2015**, all unidentified in that release. It points to the [official NAVAIR FOIA document library](https://www.navair.navy.mil/foia/documents).

M34 established the public [NAVAIR **1 - FLIR.mp4** path](https://www.navair.navy.mil/foia/sites/g/files/jejdrs566/files/2020-04/1%20-%20FLIR.mp4), also referenced by a publicly indexed DoD records release. In M35, the web reader reported **unsupported video/mp4** and a direct binary download attempt **failed**. The currently readable NAVAIR document-list HTML mentions GOFAST and GIMBAL; a search of the returned page text did **not** find `FLIR`. This is **not** proof the FOIA file was removed or that the direct URL is invalid to all browsers. No MP4 bytes, codec details or SHA-256 hash were obtained.

An additional [Wikimedia Commons FLIR1 page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:FLIR1_Official_UAP_Footage_from_the_USG_for_Public_Release.webm) provides a public **WebM/VP9 encoded, user-uploaded copy**, describes it as converted from the NAVAIR source, and lists an upload replacement at **22:24 on 27 April 2020**. The page lists **1m16s, 352×264**, about **4.77 MB**, and metadata of the **WebM derivative**, NOT the Navy MP4. The Wikimedia licence tag is a community-supplied assertion about U.S. government public-domain copyright; independently review licence/usage before publication.

**Do not claim:**
- A WebM transcode is **bit-for-bit equal** to a government MP4 (different containers/codecs alone make this untrue).
- A user-uploaded version is the original **2004 on-aircraft sensor memory**; the publicly released official file is also a later distribution copy.
- Two websites displaying the same underlying clip are **two independent sensors or witnesses**.
- Image pixels alone establish an object’s size, acceleration, identity or origin without original sensor metadata and reproducible analyses.
- An accessible third-party clip authorises background copying of an original protected third-party movie/audio track; this specifically concerns the federal Navy source media.

The same limitations remain for M33's **NASA Brief Redacted.pdf**: it is officially indexed but the direct Navy link returned **Request Rejected** again and the original PDF bytes have not been retrieved or compared with the appendix in the 212-page House submission. **Never invent an original PDF hash, full-visual audit or binary equivalence.**
