    How evidence strength is assessed (0–100 points)

    Proposed transparent editorial rubric. Only separately sourced, individually reviewed claims may receive a number. A missing evidence assessment is Not assessed, not zero. Scientific and policy recommendations are Not applicable to extraordinary-event evidence scores; their source trails remain readable but are never opened for community evidence-rating votes.

    • Primary record of the claim in context — 25 points
    • Independent corroboration of its underlying assertion — 30 points
    • Inspectible supporting records or data — 25 points
    • Specificity and independent testability — 10 points
    • Other explanations and conflicting evidence considered — 10 points

    A score describes a published evidence review, not a scientific probability. Editorial methods and individual claims can be challenged or corrected using source links.

    To suggest a primary source or challenge an assessment, contact the research desk.

          Directory policy

          Profiles are evidence guides, not popularity contests

          Statements are attributed to their speakers or published institutions. Reporting, first-hand testimony, technical analysis and alleged facts are different types of evidence. Inclusion is not an endorsement or accusation.

          We will not label real people as dishonest, part of a covert operation or otherwise malicious based on a popularity poll. The proposed evidence-strength review above applies only to a specific, attributed public claim, with a visible source trail and editorial checks. It is not a numerical rating of an individual, proof of extraordinary explanations or a public popularity poll. Community ratings will require an authenticated account and separately approved claim evidence; they are not available while editorial review and backend checks are pending. No personal believability scores are collected.

          To suggest a person, provide a public source supporting their work. To request a correction, email the research desk with the name, the disputed wording and a primary reference.
