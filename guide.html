How to research with Disclosure Desk

Start with a report, follow the original evidence, compare the historical record, build your own investigation and keep track of what is confirmed, uncertain or simply alleged. This guide explains the tools, the steps and the limits — without assuming every claim is true.

Practical walkthroughs

Four ways to investigate

Workflow A · Investigate a UAP sighting from start to finish

  1. Use News or Evidence Archive to locate the report. Record its exact title, publisher and URL.
  2. Identify the event date. Check that it is not merely the article publication date or the date of a later interview.
  3. Open the Map or case details for approximate geography and other reports. A map marker is not proof of an exact sighting position.
  4. Find the earliest original record using Sources & Archives; note which statements are firsthand and which are repeated by later publications.
  5. Check alternatives: aircraft, launches, weather, stars, imaging artefacts, mistaken dates and incomplete testimony, as appropriate.
  6. Keep citations, uncertainties and follow-up questions in a private Board or W.NE Research Studio report.
  7. Revisit the source if corrections, evidence or better explanations emerge. An unresolved report is not automatically evidence of non-human technology.

Workflow B · Trace a historical document

  1. Open Archive and filter by year, subject, country or type where available.
  2. Follow the original source link rather than relying on a headline summary.
  3. Record the archive organisation, collection, reference number, document date and access link.
  4. Distinguish the document's description of a claim from official confirmation that the claim happened.
  5. Check whether the work can be reused or republished; public viewing does not automatically grant copyright permission.

Workflow C · Build a research dossier

  1. Choose a case or question and create or open a private investigation Board.
  2. Save distinct source links with notes on credibility and missing context; avoid placing personal information into public reports.
  3. Open W.NE Research Studio to structure a longer report, sections, references or comparisons.
  4. Save deliberately, confirm where the material is stored and export a tested backup before clearing a browser or switching devices.
  5. Conclude with findings, alternative explanations, outstanding questions and a source list.

Workflow D · Record your own experience safely

  1. Open Witness. Write down what happened, the event date, approximate time if known and town/city.
  2. Leave out names, precise addresses, identifiable people and other private details.
  3. Save a private draft first, then read it again for mistakes or identifying details.
  4. Only if public publishing is enabled and you choose to do so, sign in, review the visibility choice and explicitly consent to sharing. Saving a draft never publishes it automatically.
  5. Remember that other users may challenge, comment on or report public material; stick to what you personally observed.

News: find a story and follow its source

  1. Open the News tab and browse the current feed.
  2. Use the search box and country/topic filters to narrow what you see.
  3. Choose Latest News, Top 100, or Read where displayed. Read helps find articles previously opened on that device.
  4. Open the linked publication and check its date, reporting and underlying sources.
  5. Use Archive or a Board to continue a longer investigation.

Headlines, rankings, summaries and a live ticker are discovery aids, not Disclosure Desk's verification of the underlying claim. Some links may change or expire.

Archive: go beyond the headline

The Evidence Archive collects source-linked records and case references. Search terms, year and category filters help narrow the material; the depth and source completeness vary by record.

  1. Open Archive.
  2. Filter for a specific year, location, document type or incident description.
  3. Open a case or source entry and follow its original archive reference.
  4. Write down the event date, record date and publication date separately, if known.
  5. If you want to reuse an image or document, check the record's usage terms and any necessary permission.

A mention in a government archive is evidence that the report or document exists, not proof that every interpretation within it is correct.

Map and Case Files: investigate where something happened

  1. Open the Map and search for a town, region or area.
  2. Use the available categories and controls to compare listed cases.
  3. Open a case card for its description and linked evidence.
  4. Look for similar reports while remembering that multiple reports can share the same original story or source.
  5. Use Boards to save relevant links and your own analysis.

Mapped positions are not always verified event coordinates. Witness locations are deliberately limited to town/city level, and a cluster on a map does not by itself establish a pattern or cause.

Videos, LIVE and Watch & Read

Videos

Use the Videos tab to browse credited footage and video links. Switch between browsing and swipe mode when available. Check who first released the footage, whether its date is established and whether clips have been edited.

LIVE

LIVE is for verified scheduled hearings and their associated official recordings or moderated chat where available. If nothing is scheduled, there may be no active countdown or conversation. Archived hearing chat, where published, is read-only and must not be mistaken for a live event.

Watch & Read

Explore documentaries, films, books and podcasts as leads for further research. Inclusion is not an endorsement of the views expressed by creators or authors. To recommend something, open Add a link, choose its type and enter a public link and title. A registered account is required. Valid additions appear immediately unless the same title or link is already listed. Use Report for inappropriate content: five reports from different registered members hide an item for owner review. An optional note is private; the title and link are public.

W.NE Research Studio: turn sources into a report

W.NE Research Edition is Disclosure Desk's investigation-oriented writing workspace. It is related to, but distinct from, the original W.NE writing product by Boyd Digital Studios.

  1. Open the W.NE tab.
  2. If signed in, work with your own documents and available private research tools. Guests may see a read-only demonstration; they cannot open other users' research.
  3. Structure sections around what happened, chronology, evidence, citations, alternative explanations and findings.
  4. Use existing note, comparison, citation and export tools only where offered by your account/workspace.
  5. Check save confirmation, export results and backups before trusting the system with irreplaceable records.

Keep a copy you can open anywhere

Choose File → Download Word copy for an ordinary .docx document you can open in Word or another compatible editor. Emergency Copy also includes your current writing as a Word file when a cloud save is unavailable. Backup Library downloads a ZIP containing Word copies plus W.NE recovery files. For an earlier draft, open Recovery History and download a Word copy or recover a separate document. Existing reports are not overwritten.

When configured, File → Google Drive lets you manually back up a Word copy to your own Google account or open a previous backup as a new document. The panel explains if setup is still needed. Google Drive is optional; local Word downloads work without it. Word copies preserve supported document content; W.NE recovery files retain application-specific data.

Privacy caution: Some writing assistance sends text to a secured LanguageTool service. Read the Privacy Policy before submitting sensitive material. Research Edition is in beta and should not be the sole copy of essential research.

Investigation Boards: keep your leads organised

  1. Open Boards and select My Boards or the case-related tools.
  2. Create a Board for a specific investigation or question.
  3. Save articles, source links and private notes as you research.
  4. Mark what the source actually establishes and what remains unconfirmed.
  5. Make a separate backup of important material before deleting app data, clearing browser storage or switching phones.

Important: Some Board material is stored in your current browser. Do not assume that every Board or local note automatically synchronises across devices or is recoverable after browser data is cleared.

Research Inbox

  1. In Chrome or another Android app, open the source you want to keep. Tap Share and choose Disclosure Desk Beta M92.
  2. After sharing, give the link a recognisable name in the Android prompt (for example, “Pentagon hearing · witness statement”). You can tap Later and name it afterwards.

Links stay in this app installation on your phone. You can save them without signing in. They don’t sync to your account or another device, and uninstalling the beta or clearing its app data erases them.

Witness: record an observation without exposing your identity

Witness lets you save an account of your experience privately. Public submission requires the appropriate enabled feature, a registered account and your explicit consent.

  1. Provide the event category, date and approximate time if you know it.
  2. Enter town/city and broad region rather than a home address, street or GPS coordinate.
  3. Describe what you saw without identifying another witness or including contact information.
  4. Save a private draft, check it, then make a separate decision about publishing if that option is enabled.
  5. Read the Community Rules before sharing. Public witness reports are subject to limits and moderation.
The public witness-submission function is feature-controlled. A button being visible does not guarantee that your account can publish right now. Private drafts should be backed up where important.

Chat and Discussions: connect with researchers

Private one-to-one Chat

  1. Sign in, open Chat and select New chat. Search an exact public username. Names chosen at signup become public and searchable by default when members first sign in, unless they hide them in Profile.
  2. Send an introductory request; the recipient must accept before you can exchange messages.
  3. Use the conversation list, private message view and conversation drawer. Unread counts appear where supported.
  4. Use the safety menu for reporting or blocking. Chat is for text, including phone-keyboard emoji; it is not currently an image/GIF upload platform.

Community Discussions

  1. Open Discussions and choose a subject. Guests can read; sign in to contribute.
  2. Registered eligible accounts can create a subject, subject to account-age and rate limits.
  3. Open a subject, choose New post, add a title and mark it Serious or Fun. Add an image URL or source reference where supported; external image hosts may block previews.
  4. Open a post to read its comments, add your own or reply. Use the arrows to like or dislike, or save it to a Board and share its link.
  5. Use the flag to report harmful content. Subject stewards organise and escalate; site-wide moderation remains separately authorised.
Privacy: The first Chat message is a request, not an accepted conversation. Public usernames can receive Chat requests by default, but people must accept to continue. Use Profile to hide your username or turn off requests. Never share sensitive personal information in public posts.

Satellite Research: compare the case date with real historical imagery

Satellite Research is being tested inside the Archive rather than as another main navigation tab. It links public, dated cases to official satellite research tools. It does not claim a UFO appears in any returned image.

  1. In the current phone beta, open Archive → Satellite Research and choose a public historical case.
  2. Review the case's recorded event date and approximate mapped area. If the original only supplies a year, no arbitrary day should be assumed.
  3. Enter a UTC time only if you have it from a reliable record; otherwise leave it unknown. Noon is sometimes used as a browsing anchor, not a sighting time.
  4. For compatible modern dates, NASA Worldview can open with the approximate region and selected calendar date. Imagery availability depends on the actual dataset and acquisition.
  5. For older dates, USGS EarthExplorer can open with an approximate public location and Landsat MSS dataset; manually set the suggested acquisition date range and inspect actual scene metadata.
  6. EUMETSAT historical viewers may require you to select the calendar date manually. The app does not promise their public links accept arbitrary event-date prefills.
  7. Use Copy case search details for the prepared date, coordinates and suggested USGS range. Keep the actual satellite acquisition timestamp separate from the event's reported time.
Coverage matters: Landsat MSS ground imagery starts in 1972 but is occasional daylight imagery, not continuous video; the EUMETSAT historic gallery begins in 1981; many NASA Worldview daily layers date from about 2000 onward. The beta has not verified that a matching nearest historical frame exists for any particular report. An image with nothing visible does not rule out the sighting.

Only rounded coordinates from public, mapped cases should go to external providers. Witness drafts, precise witness addresses and your current device location are not passed.

Frequently asked questions

Find an answer quickly

The search near the top of this page filters these questions and the how-to topics. You can also browse every question below.

Is Disclosure Desk a government or official UAP agency website?

No. Disclosure Desk is an independent research project by Boyd Digital Studios. It links to original government, academic and independent materials without claiming affiliation or endorsement.

Does being in an official archive prove a UFO or non-human craft existed?

No. An authentic document can prove that a record, investigation or allegation existed. Whether the reported observation is accurate, adequately explained or extraordinary requires separate evidence.

Do I need an account to research cases?

No account is required for ordinary public browsing such as News, Archive, Map, Videos and the source directory. Private saved documents, public posting and some account settings require sign-in or an enabled feature.

Where do I start if I am completely new?

Open Explore and run the full site tour, then use News, Archive and Map to investigate one report. Work through Workflow A before drawing conclusions.

Can I search by a person's name, year, place or case title?

Use the filters and search boxes in the appropriate page. Available search fields differ between News, Archive, Map and the source directory; a missing search hit does not prove no record exists elsewhere.

How do I find the original source rather than another summary?

Follow the article's publisher link or the archived case's cited reference. The Sources & Archives directory links to primary collections. Record the reference number, organisation, date and page when possible.

Why do two reports sometimes look like separate sightings?

Different websites may repeat the same primary document, report or footage. Compare first publication, location, event date and cited witnesses before treating two entries as independent evidence.

Why is an older article or video link broken?

External publishers sometimes remove, rename or restrict their pages. Search the cited publisher or archive using the title and reference number. A broken link is not evidence that its content was suppressed.

Can I save my research and come back later?

Use Boards for saved sources and notes; W.NE Research Edition offers additional document tools. Some material is local to your browser. Save deliberately, test an export or backup, and do not assume every item syncs across devices.

How do I save a link from Chrome to Research Inbox?

In Chrome, tap Share → Disclosure Desk Beta M92. You can name the link when it arrives, or choose Later. Then tap Research Inbox from the Desk menu, News or Explore; open its ⋯ menu and select Rename to change its title any time. The Android beta keeps these links on your phone. See the Research Inbox guide for all the steps.

Are Boards shared publicly?

Investigation Boards and private research start private. Do not assume a Board is public or automatically synced. If a future feature offers sharing, check its specific visibility and confirmation settings first.

Can I recover Boards after clearing my browser or changing phone?

Not necessarily. Some Boards and notes are stored in the current browser. Clearing site data can permanently erase local content, so make separate copies of important material before a device change.

Can I export a W.NE report as a document?

Research Edition provides save and export tools where enabled, but supported output types depend on the active workspace and release. Check the offered menu and open the exported file before relying on it; don't assume every format is available.

Is my work private in W.NE Research Studio?

Research items are private by default. Check the privacy and storage controls for each tool. Some writing-assistance requests may send selected text through the secured LanguageTool service; see the Privacy Policy.

Can I report a sighting anonymously?

You can save a private draft without public exposure. Public publishing, when enabled, requires sign-in and an explicit choice; the form offers an Anonymous display option. Never include exact addresses or identity clues in the report body.

Will Disclosure Desk show my actual GPS location?

Public witness reporting is designed around town/city-level information. You should not provide precise coordinates or addresses in public descriptions. Beta satellite links use approximate public case coordinates, not your device geolocation.

How many witness reports can I publish?

When public submissions are enabled, the published rules specify three new reports per rolling 24 hours for an eligible registered account. Private drafts are separate and are never auto-published.

What is the difference between a dislike and reporting a problem?

Likes and dislikes express an opinion. Formal flags or reports are moderation signals for rule violations. The rules describe the thresholds and review process; disagreement alone is not grounds to silence someone.

Can anyone post in Community Discussions today?

Guests can read public Discussions. Eligible registered members can create subjects, posts and replies on the public website, subject to account eligibility, rate limits and moderation rules.

How does private Chat work, and is it public?

Chat is a private one-to-one feature for registered members. Newly chosen usernames are discoverable after first sign-in by default, and members can switch that off in Profile. Send an introductory request to an exact public username; the recipient must accept before messaging continues. Chat is separate from public Discussions.

Can I send photos, GIFs or videos in Chat?

The current beta Chat supports text, including standard Unicode emoji from your keyboard. Native attachments, GIF/sticker uploads and voice notes are not currently supported.

Are reports and community posts moderated?

Enabled public submissions and community content are subject to account eligibility, rate limits, reporting and server-side moderation. Section stewards have limited organising powers; they are not unrestricted site administrators.

Why do my case and a satellite image show different times?

The event time is what a witness reported; the acquisition time is when a satellite recorded an image. They are different observations and may not coincide. Never interpret a date-selected viewer as a verified picture of the incident.

Can Satellite Research show Arizona on 3 June 1976 automatically?

In the beta, the case can provide the approximate area, event date and USGS Landsat MSS dataset selection where supported. EarthExplorer still requires a manual acquisition-date range. You must inspect actual scene coverage and times. No matching satellite photograph is guaranteed.

Why doesn't NASA Worldview show imagery for an older 1970s case?

The beta's NASA MODIS daily layer starts around 2000. For a 1970s case, use the separate USGS Landsat MSS archive search beginning in 1972; it is not continuous weather video or a guaranteed observation of the event.

Why doesn't the EUMETSAT link open on the exact historical day?

Its historical gallery has no verified automatic case-date link for this workflow. Choose a date manually and check whether the archive has a frame, its actual timestamp and geographic coverage.

Does a blank satellite image disprove a witness account?

No. Coverage, clouds, image resolution, daylight, acquisition time and the satellite's viewing angle all matter. A feature may not be resolvable or may have occurred between acquisitions.

Can I install Disclosure Desk on my phone?

On browsers that support it, use the browser menu's Add to Home screen or Install app option. This creates an app-like shortcut; it is not the same as a native Play Store or App Store release.

Does the entire website work offline?

No. Current news, external archives, video and authenticated functions generally need a network connection. Some local notes may remain in browser storage, but do not assume full offline availability or automatic cloud backups.

Does the website charge for reading research?

Public browsing of the ordinary research sections does not require payment. External websites may have their own registration, access or licensing restrictions. This guide does not promise that all off-site source material is free.

May I reuse or publish an archive image in my project?

Check the original item and collection's rights statement. Access to a government archive or public website is not blanket reproduction permission. Contact the holding archive or rights holder if necessary.

How can I correct an error or suggest a source?

Email support@uapdisclosuredesk.com with the case title, relevant source link and a concise explanation. Do not include passwords or identifying witness details.

Interpret evidence carefully

Research glossary

UAP
Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena. An observational classification, not an established explanation or origin.
Primary source
The original witness account, document, recording or dataset rather than a later summary.
Provenance
Where a claim, image or document came from, when it was produced and how it was handled.
Event date vs publication date
The day the reported event occurred versus when a record or article was created. These must not be silently interchanged.
Acquisition timestamp
When a satellite or sensor collected data. It may be very different from a reported sighting time.
Hypothesis
A possible explanation to test against the evidence, not a statement already proved.
Unresolved
Insufficient reliable evidence to make a confident classification, not proof of something extraordinary.

Our guiding principle: Keep an open mind, check the original evidence and say clearly what you do not yet know.
